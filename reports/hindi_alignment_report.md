# Uttara Kāṇḍa — Hindi alignment audit (`data_7.json` vs Gita Press `.txt`)

**Report only.** Nothing in `data_7.json` or any `.txt` was modified; only this report and its JSON twin were written.

## Method, assumptions & thresholds (read first)

**What is being tested.** For each `data_7.json` row that carries real Hindi, does that Hindi translate the *same* verse as the Sanskrit sitting on the row? The Hindi and the Sanskrit were both drafted from the Gita Press edition (the `.txt` files), so each row is matched **back to the `.txt`**: its Hindi → the `.txt` verse it came from (`V_hi`), its Sanskrit → the `.txt` verse it starts in (`V_sk`). A row **drifts** when `V_hi ≠ V_sk`.

**Why match the `.txt`, not `data_7`'s own neighbours.** An earlier statistics-only pass (comparing each row's Hindi to its neighbours *within* `data_7`) failed: continuous dialogue gives adjacent verses heavy lexical overlap. Matching the fixed `.txt` source is decisive — the true verse scores ≈1.0, the nearest rival ≈0.3.

**Why `V_hi` vs `V_sk` and not vs the shloka number.** Gita Press and the Tokunaga text used by `data_7` split half-verses (pādas) at different points, and `data_7` also carries extra split rows (e.g. `51.4.1`). So a row's shloka number is **not** comparable to a `.txt` verse number. `V_sk` (from the row's *first* pāda) gives a frame-independent reference: it is the `.txt` verse the row's Sanskrit begins in.

**Matching.** `difflib.SequenceMatcher(autojunk=False)`; score = `max(ratio, containment)` where containment = matched-chars / len(query). Text normalised before comparison: `{hN}`/`{sN}` markers, verse stamps (`॥N॥`, `।।7.s.v।।`), quotes, brackets and dashes, avagraha, then all daṇḍas and whitespace stripped. Devanagari digits → Latin.

**Confidence gates.** Hindi match trusted when best ≥ **0.60** *and* (best − 2nd) ≥ **0.08**; Sanskrit reference trusted when best ≥ **0.60**. A row is only reported as *drift* when **both** are confident and `V_hi ≠ V_sk`. Rows failing either gate are listed separately as **low-confidence** (not asserted as drift).

**Offset.** `offset = V_hi − V_sk`, signed: `+1` = Hindi belongs to the *next* verse, `−1` = the *previous* verse. Colophon rows (Sanskrit `इत्यार्षे…`) have no verse Sanskrit; their reference is taken as *last-verse + 1*, so a colophon carrying the final verse's Hindi reads as `−1`.

## Task 1 — parse coverage

- `.txt` Hindi/Sanskrit extracted for sargas **1-111** (111 sargas); `data_7.json` covers sargas **1-111** (111). Both sides span the full Uttara Kāṇḍa — no sarga is scoped out.
- **Unparseable verse translations: 0.** Every Hindi block that follows a Sanskrit verse received a verse number (from its `{hN}` marker, its `॥N॥` stamp, or the current verse), so no verse translation was lost.
- 71 sarga **topic-heading** lines (the Gita Press content-summary printed under each sarga title, before verse 1) carry no verse number and were skipped — they are not verse translations. Devanagari verse numbers were converted to Latin throughout.
