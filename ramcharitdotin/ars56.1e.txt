Verses 1–26, English translation for 56.1, Prachipt sarga.
Synopsis: "By Brahma's command, Indra, king of the gods, goes to Lanka together with the goddess Nidra (Sleep), offers Sita a portion of divine kheer, and returns after taking leave of her."

Disclaimer: "This chapter fits well with the surrounding narrative and is of good quality. It is published with a translation in some manuscript editions too, but classical Sanskrit commentaries such as the Tilaka are not available for it; for this reason, some scholars consider it an interpolation. Since it is useful, it is published here as well, along with its translation."

1. When Sita had been brought into Lanka, the Grandsire Brahma then spoke thus to the well-pleased Indra, lord of a hundred sacrifices:
2. "O king of the gods, for the welfare of the three worlds and for the destruction of the rakshasas, the evil-souled Ravana has brought Sita into Lanka.
3–3½. The illustrious and devoted wife Janaki, who had always lived happily, now finds herself deprived of the sight of her husband and sees only rakshasa-women before her. Surrounded by rakshasa-women, she yearns intensely for the sight of her lord.
4–4½. The city of Lanka is situated on the shore of the ocean. How then will Rama come to know that the blameless Sita is held there?
5–6. Sunk in grief and lost in many anxious thoughts, she has become extremely hard to find. Taking no sustenance to keep herself alive, she will surely give up her life. Should Sita's life come to an end, our purpose would once again fall into the same doubt as before.
7.Therefore, go quickly from here, enter the city of Lanka, see the fair-faced Sita, and give her this excellent offering."
8.Thus addressed, the illustrious Indra, chastiser of Paka, then came, together with Nidra, the goddess of Sleep, to the city ruled by Ravana.
9–9½. He said to Nidra, "Go and cast the rakshasas into deep sleep." Thus commanded by Indra, the goddess was greatly delighted, and to accomplish the purpose of the gods, she cast the rakshasas into a deep, bewildering sleep.
10–10½. Meanwhile, the thousand-eyed god, Shachi's lord, approached her as she sat in the grove, and spoke to her these words:
11–12. "I am the king of the gods; may all be well with you. I have come here, O you of the pure smile. I shall render assistance toward the fulfilment of noble Raghava's purpose — do not grieve, O daughter of Janaka.
13. By my grace, he will cross the ocean together with his forces. And O auspicious one, it is I who have bewildered these rakshasa-women here by my power of illusion.
14. Therefore, O Sita, I myself have brought this food, this sacred offering, and have come to you, O Vaidehi, together with Nidra, having sought you out.
15. If you eat this from my hand, O auspicious one, hunger and thirst will not trouble you even for ten thousand years."
16. Thus addressed, she, filled with doubt, said to Indra: "How am I to know that you, standing here, are indeed Indra, Shachi's lord?
17. I have seen the marks of divinity in the presence of Rama and Lakshmana. Show me those same signs, O Indra, if you are indeed the king of the gods himself."
18–19. Hearing Sita's words, Shachi's lord did just that: his feet did not touch the earth, his eyes did not blink, the garments he wore gathered no dust, and the flowers he wore did not wilt. Recognizing Vasava by these signs, Sita became greatly delighted.
20. Weeping, she spoke these words concerning Raghava: "O Lord, by good fortune, news of the mighty-armed one, together with his brother, has reached my ears today.
21. Just as King Dasharatha is my father-in-law, and just as the lord of Mithila is my own father, so I regard you today; through you, my husband now has a protector.
22. By your command, O Indra, I shall eat this milk-offering that you have given me — may it bring increase to the line of Raghu."
23. Taking that rice-pudding from Indra's hand, Maithili of the pure smile mentally dedicated it first to her husband and to Lakshmana, and said:
24. "If my mighty husband lives, together with his brother, may this be offered to the two of them with devotion." Having said this, she herself then ate the rice-pudding.
25. Having eaten that offering, the fair-faced one thus cast off the suffering born of hunger and thirst; and having received news of the two Kakutsthas from Indra, Janaki became joyful at heart.
26. And Shakra too, pleased, then went to his own abode in the heavens, for the accomplishment of Raghava's purpose; taking leave of Sita, the noble one went, together with Nidra, to his own dwelling.
